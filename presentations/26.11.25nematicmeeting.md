# Overview

- Why consider **nematic order** in osteogenic systems?
- Nematic defects are points where the orientation can’t remain continuous, defined by the winding of the director field.
- In cell layers, they form from collective alignment and organise flows, stress, and local differentiation.
- Take-home: **defects and LC microenvironments act as functional regulators** of osteogenesis

# Nematic defects?

- **m (defect charge):** how many half-turns the director makes around the core (e.g., +½, –½, +1).
- **k (sign):** direction of rotation — positive = anticlockwise, negative = clockwise.
- **θ(r):** local director angle describing molecular/cellular orientation.
- **Angle rule:** circling once around the defect changes the angle by **Δθ = 2π m**, defining the defect type.

# 1) Yang et al., 2025  

## Topological defects regulate MSC osteogenesis



- Human MSC monolayers form **+1/2 and −1/2 defects**
- Defect geometry drives **centripetal / centrifugal migration**
- Stress concentration at defect cores → **heterogeneous proliferation and osteogenic differentiation**
- Suggested activation of **RUNX2 / SOX9** via local mechanical cues
